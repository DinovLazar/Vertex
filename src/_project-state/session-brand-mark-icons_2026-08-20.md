# Session — Brand mark: VERTEX disc replaces the "V" chevron (2026-08-20)

**Ask:** "add this as the tab/link photo instead of the V" — with a 1024×1024 PNG
of the new mark: a black disc carrying the VERTEX wordmark in white.

**Scope:** every surface that rendered the old geometric "V" chevron — the tab
favicon, the Apple touch icon, the PWA manifest icons, the schema.org
`Organization.logo`, and the logo badge on the Open Graph / link-preview card.
The site's own header and footer already used the word "VERTEX" as live text
(no chevron anywhere in the chrome), so the new mark makes the icon set
*consistent* with the chrome rather than diverging from it. No component
changed.

---

## What the old mark was

`src/app/icon.svg` and `public/logo.svg` each hand-held a 12-point path — a
chevron "V" drawn with `stroke-linejoin: round` on a dark tile:

```
M20 24 L38 24 L50 54 L62 24 L80 24 L50 76 Z
```

`icon.svg` put it on a `rx="22"` rounded tile with a `#1C1C1C → #0E0E0E`
gradient; `logo.svg` put the same path on a flat `#141414` square. The rasters
(`favicon.ico`, `apple-icon.png`, `icon-192.png`, `icon-512.png`, `logo.png`)
were baked from those by hand at some earlier point, with no script to
regenerate them and no shared definition — two copies of the path, five
untracked derivations.

## What replaced it

One vector definition in **`src/lib/brand-mark.ts`** — two glyphs (`wordmark`,
`monogram`) on two plates (`disc`, `bleed`) — and a generator,
**`scripts/build-brand-icons.ts`**, that emits every other copy from it. The
two `.svg` files are now generated artefacts carrying a
`<!-- Generated by … Do not edit by hand -->` header.

### Getting a vector out of a raster

The mark arrived as a raster only — 1024×1024, flat `#0E0E0E` disc, `#F5F5F5`
wordmark, hard-edged (just 104 anti-aliased pixels in the whole image), no live
text and no source vector. Measured off the master:

- disc: centre (512, 512), radius 512 — full-bleed, verified circular to
  ±0.3px by half-chord sampling at six scanlines
- wordmark bbox: x 146–878, y 447–574 (733 × 127), centred at x 512, optically
  centred at y 510

Rather than embed a PNG in the SVG, the white channel was traced back to
vectors: pixel-boundary edges chained into closed loops (most-clockwise turn at
diagonal pinch points, so 8-connected glyphs stay one loop), then
Douglas-Peucker simplified.

**The tolerance is the interesting part.** A rasterised straight edge wanders
up to ~1px off the true line, so its staircase corners sit *just under* 1px
from the ideal — 0.95px for the 1:3 diagonals in the V and X. Any tolerance
below that faithfully reproduces the staircase, which is an artefact, not
design. Tolerances tried:

| eps  | path bytes | pixels differing from the master |
|------|-----------:|---------------------------------:|
| 0.5  |      6,631 |                               41 |
| 0.9  |      2,847 |                              301 |
| 1.1  |      1,007 |                              495 |
| **1.2** | **743** |                          **531** |
| 1.7  |        607 |                              656 |

**1.2 shipped.** It sits just above the staircase amplitude, so straight edges
collapse to two points while the R's bowl keeps its curvature. 7 loops, 743
bytes, and a re-render that differs from the master by 531 of 1,048,576 pixels
(0.05%) — all of it sub-pixel edge noise, i.e. *disagreement with the
rasterisation*, not with the artwork. Side-by-side renders at 744px are
indistinguishable. Note the metric is measured against the noisy master, so a
lower number is not automatically better; eps 0.5's 41 mismatches are the
reward for reproducing the master's own jaggies.

The R's counter is a separate loop rather than a reverse-wound subpath, so the
path needs `fill-rule="evenodd"`.

### disc vs bleed

Two shapes come out of the same wordmark, because alpha survives on the web and
does not survive mobile icon masks:

- **`disc`** — `#0E0E0E` circle, transparent outside it. For anywhere the host
  surface shows through and may be light or dark: browser tabs, `purpose:
  "any"` manifest icons, the knowledge-panel logo. Reads correctly on Chrome's
  light (`#DEE1E6`) and dark (`#202124`) tab strips and on a white SERP panel.
- **`bleed`** — same wordmark, full-bleed `#0E0E0E` square, no circle. For
  platforms that impose their own silhouette and discard alpha: iOS
  squircle-masks the Apple touch icon and paints transparency black, and
  Android crops `purpose: "maskable"` to its own shape. A disc nested inside
  either mask reads as a mistake.

The wordmark's corners sit 372px from centre on the 1024 grid, inside the
409.6px radius of Android's 80% maskable safe zone, so `bleed` needs no extra
inset — it is the disc variant with the plate squared off, nothing rescaled.

## Follow-up (same session) — the V monogram at favicon sizes

A six-letter wordmark cannot be read in 16 pixels: cap height lands at ~2px and
"VERTEX" renders as a light smear across a dark disc. This was flagged as a
limitation, and Goran asked for the V-only variant. It shipped.

### The glyph

`MONOGRAM_PATH` is **a slice of `WORDMARK_PATH`, not a second drawing** — the
first of the seven traced loops, the wordmark's own V, in the same letterform
and the same typeface. `WORDMARK_PATH` is now composed as
`MONOGRAM_PATH + WORDMARK_TAIL_PATH`, so the V exists exactly once in the file
and the two marks cannot drift. Proof it is byte-identical to what shipped an
hour earlier: every wordmark artefact came out of the regenerated build at
exactly its previous size (apple-icon 2,074 · icon-192 4,400 · icon-512 12,868
· maskable 5,464 · logo.png 12,868).

Note this is **not** the pre-2026-08-20 chevron coming back. That was a
hand-drawn 12-point path with rounded joins and no relationship to the
wordmark. This is the actual VERTEX V — flat-cut, sharp joins.

### The scale

Rendered at 44 / 50 / 55 / 60% cap height, at 16px and 32px, and looked at.
**55% shipped** (`MONOGRAM_CAP_RATIO = 560/1024`):

- stem lands at **1.93px** on a 16px favicon — thick enough to survive
  anti-aliasing as a letter rather than a grey suggestion
- arm tips sit 388px from centre on the 1024 grid, inside the 512px disc
- 60% crowds the disc edge; 50% goes thin and timid at 16px

Applied as an SVG `transform` (scale about the V's bbox centre, re-centre on
the disc) rather than baked coordinates, so `MONOGRAM_PATH` stays a literal
slice of the wordmark.

### Why the split is per-surface, not per-size

The intuitive implementation — monogram in the 16 and 32 ICO frames, wordmark
everywhere else — **would have changed nothing in any current browser.** Next
emits two icon links:

```html
<link rel="icon" href="/favicon.ico" sizes="48x48" type="image/x-icon">
<link rel="icon" href="/icon.svg"    sizes="any"   type="image/svg+xml">
```

Chrome, Firefox and Safari all prefer the SVG. An SVG favicon can respond to
`prefers-color-scheme`, but it has no way to know what size it is being painted
at — there is no container or size query in that context — so one file cannot
carry both glyphs. Leaving `icon.svg` on the wordmark would have meant the
monogram never appeared in a tab at all.

So the line is drawn by **surface**:

| Surface | Glyph |
|---------|-------|
| `src/app/icon.svg`, all three `favicon.ico` frames (16/32/48) | monogram |
| `apple-icon.png` 180, manifest 192/512/maskable, `logo.png`, `logo.svg`, the OG badge | wordmark |

All three ICO frames match, including 48, for the same reason: some consumers —
Google's SERP favicon among them — fetch the largest frame and downscale it
themselves, which would have put the unreadable wordmark straight back into a
16px slot.

Every surface that still shows the wordmark renders at 180px or above, where
six letters resolve cleanly.
